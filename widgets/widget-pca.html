How to play

The link carries 2 numbers per frame, not the 10 joint angles the motion capture records. A dancer performs in one city while a projection, the echo, dances the same routine in another.

Tracks are movement patterns from the recording, and each sends one number per frame. The percentage on a track is how much of the motion it keeps on its own.

Send a track by dragging it into the link, or press it. Press it again to take it off. When both tracks are full, a new track replaces track 2.

The echo follows at once, over the original’s shadow. With no tracks it holds the routine’s average pose. Keep at least 75% of the motion to put it in step.

The ledger under the meter splits the routine’s total variance into the variance the tracks capture and the reconstruction error the echo is left with.

The strip under the dancers shows the motion matched at each moment. Drag along it, or use ← and →, to hold a moment. Pause stops the dancers.

Why the two biggest tracks don’t add up

PCA’s tracks never repeat each other. Each is at right angles to the rest (orthogonal), so the motion they keep adds up exactly.

This connects to principal component analysis in Deep Learning. The course widget turns a direction through a cloud of two-number points; here each frame has ten joint angles and the link keeps two.

In the lecture’s terms, the link is an encoder and the echo is a decoder. Each frame’s ten joint angles are projected onto the two tracks on the link, which gives two numbers per frame, and the echo maps those two numbers back to a pose. Motion kept is the variance those two directions capture. What the echo gets wrong, squared and averaged over the frames, is the reconstruction error.

The motion the echo misses is the part of each pose at right angles to the tracks on the link. Put a single track on the link and read the ledger, then try three different pairs and add up the two numbers each time. What does the ledger tell you about the pair that captures the most?

The routine is synthesized for this puzzle from eight movement patterns; no motion capture or trained model is involved. Each frame has ten joint angles. Motion is how far the joints move from the routine’s average pose, with each joint weighted by how much of the body it moves: a degree of torso lean counts four times as much as a degree at the elbow. Each track is a fixed pattern of joint movement. For every frame, the link sends the two numbers that bring the echo as close as possible to the original (least squares). Motion kept is the share of the original’s variation that the echo reproduces. The principal components are computed from the routine’s 240 frames on your device. Every pair that meets the brief counts.
