YOUR DELIVERIES

How to play

The markers are nine measurements of a forest trail hidden by mist. The note above the map says how each trail was measured.

The route starts as a straight line from the depot to the delivery hut. Each bend lets it change direction once.

Add a bend by tapping the route, up to four, then drag it left or right. The route refits to the markers each time. With a keyboard, Tab to a bend, move it with the arrow keys and remove it with Delete.

Send the rover to lock the route. The mist lifts as it drives, showing the trail and an extra survey. Aim for an average miss of 2 m or less.

Three trails follow, each measured differently. After a delivery you can replay it, explore the revealed trail, or move on.

The idea behind the game

WHAT THE TRAIL SHOWS

Each bend is one more way to change direction.

A bend near a real turn lets the route follow the trail. A bend anywhere else still brings the route closer to the markers, because it can follow their measurement errors. That is why the distance to the markers keeps falling as you add bends, while the distance to the trail can rise.

The extra survey was measured the same way as the markers but played no part in the fit. Its distance tends to rise and fall with the trail's, which is why separate measurements are used to choose between routes.

In the course, this connects to approximation, generalization, and using separate data to choose and evaluate a model.

How this small simulation works

All scenery and measurements are synthetic. The route is pinned to the depot and the delivery hut. You choose where its bends go; the size of each turn is fitted to the markers by least squares. A network with one hidden layer of ReLU units draws exactly this kind of route, one bend per unit.

Average miss is the mean north-south distance between route and trail at 401 evenly spaced positions, measured on the underlying simulated trail rather than another noisy sample. The rover animation illustrates this score; it does not simulate vehicle dynamics. Once the trail is revealed, further changes are labeled exploration.
