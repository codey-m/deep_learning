What is this model learning?

This is a simplified example with designed data. In the first table, each row is a word and each column is a context the word can appear near. A 1 means the word always appears with that context, and 0 means never. These rows are the training data, so they stay the same at every epoch.

The model squeezes each row into three numbers, which are the word’s vector in the plot. A decoder then tries to rebuild the whole row from those three numbers. Training adjusts both until the rebuilt rows match the supplied rows as closely as three numbers allow. The second table shows the rebuilt rows at the current epoch, so it changes as you train.

Reconstruction error at this epoch, the average squared gap between the two tables: .

Real embeddings are learned from how words co-occur in large amounts of text, and they use far more numbers per word. Word2vec embeddings typically have a few hundred; the widely used pretrained word2vec vectors have 300.
