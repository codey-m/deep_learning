Projection onto a direction

Some representation-learning methods encode a point by its coordinate along a direction, then decode it by going back along that direction. This page checks that move in two dimensions, with one unit direction.

About 3 minutes if you remember it. About 10 if you use the reminder.

    Show the full worked explanation
    1. Because v has length 1, the coordinate of x along it is the dot product: z = 0.6·3 + 0.8·1 = 2.6.
    2. Decoding goes back along v: x̂ = 2.6 · (0.6, 0.8) = (1.56, 2.08). Every reconstruction from one coordinate lies on the line through v.
    3. The leftover is x − x̂ = (3 − 1.56, 1 − 2.08) = (1.44, −1.08). Its squared length is 1.44² + 1.08² = 3.24.
    4. A check: the leftover is at a right angle to v, since (1.44)(0.6) + (−1.08)(0.8) = 0. That right angle is what makes x̂ the closest point on the line to x.
